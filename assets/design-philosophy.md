# Registration Drift

**A visual philosophy.**

---

Two impressions of the same form, laid one atop the other, will never agree perfectly. The gap between them is not failure. It is the only place where truth becomes visible. Registration Drift takes that gap as its entire subject: the hairline of difference between what was intended and what arrived, held still long enough to be looked at. The work does not illustrate the difference. It **is** the difference, rendered with the patience of someone who has spent years learning that a tenth of a millimetre is a decision.

Space is organised as a plate, not a page. A single ground of warm paper, held quiet by margins wide enough to feel expensive, and within it one dense field where all the incident lives. The field is built from repetition: rules stacked at measured intervals, ticks accumulating along an edge, marks that reward the eye for staying. Nothing is scattered. Everything sits on a structure so exact that a viewer feels the grid without ever seeing it drawn. This is the hardest kind of composition to execute and the easiest to get wrong, and the work must show, unmistakably, that it was executed by a hand at the top of its discipline.

Colour is withheld almost entirely. A ground the colour of uncoated stock, a near-black that is never quite black, and one saturated accent used the way a surgeon uses a mark: rarely, deliberately, and only where something must be named. The accent never decorates. It appears exactly where the two impressions fail to agree, and its quantity on the page is therefore an honest measurement rather than a styling choice. Where the accent is thick, something is wrong. Where it vanishes, the register is true. The restraint here must read as conviction, not timidity, and achieving that distinction takes painstaking calibration.

Scale runs to extremes with nothing in the middle. One typographic gesture large enough to carry the composition, and everything else reduced to clinical annotation set at the threshold of comfortable reading, letter-spaced wide, arranged in columns that align to the same invisible structure as the marks. There are no paragraphs. Text appears as label, index, unit and coordinate, the vocabulary of an instrument rather than an essay. Each word must earn its position through countless small adjustments of tracking and placement, the kind of labour that is invisible when done well and glaring when skipped.

Rhythm comes from accumulation, not variation. Bands repeat. Ticks repeat. Indices count upward in a steady hand. Against that regularity, a single deviation becomes enormous: one rule that sits lower than its neighbours reads as an event, and the whole composition bends toward it. This is the movement's central mechanism, and it only works if the repetition is flawless. A single misaligned mark destroys the effect, which is why the execution must be obsessive, verified, and reverified, the product of deep expertise rather than a confident first attempt.

The finished artefact should feel recovered rather than designed: a plate from a discipline that does not exist, produced by an institution that measured something ephemeral with the same seriousness others reserve for the movement of planets. It should look as though it took an unreasonable number of hours, because it did. Master-level execution is not a flourish added at the end. It is the entire argument.
